## Train all eye movements

Stereo Reader began as a parallel-view reader designed to train divergence and voluntary focusing. Since then, it has evolved into a much more comprehensive visual eye-training environment, with programmable exercises covering a wider range of eye movements and extraocular-muscle work.

The main training system is built around **dynamic modulations**. Instead of simply holding the eyes in one position, Stereo Reader continuously changes the visual field in controlled patterns. These patterns can train **divergence, cyclovergence, cycloversion, vertical and horizontal movements, and combinations of several movements at once**.

This extends training beyond static stereo viewing or a single pair of muscles. Different modulation patterns can involve the muscles responsible for moving the eyes inward and outward, up and down, horizontally, and rotationally, providing a more complete range of eye-muscle exercise.

Earlier versions of the method relied more heavily on active stereo reading, where the user maintained parallel view while reading text near the limit of visual clarity. This can provide strong training, but it also requires sustained attention and deliberate visual effort. Dynamic modulations make training much easier to reproduce: the application provides the changing visual stimulus automatically while the user simply follows or watches it.

The goal is to improve **flexibility, coordination, and the usable range of eye movement** through short, repeatable sessions without requiring the user to consciously control individual muscles or learn complicated exercise sequences. Stereo reading remains an important part of the application, both as a parallel-view reading mode and as a training method, but **programmable modulation training is now the most advanced and direct eye-training functionality in Stereo Reader**.
